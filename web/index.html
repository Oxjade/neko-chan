What has to be true before real money

A high win rate over a few trades is luck. Neko moves from paper to live trading only when all four of these checks pass, and the win-rate check uses the low end of the likely range, so a short streak cannot pass it.

A win rate also means nothing without the payoff beside it. Winning 70% of trades loses money if the losses are three times the size of the wins. That is why this page shows the break-even line and not a single headline percentage.

Replaying the engine on past prices

How a trade happens

  1. Read the market

    Every few minutes the agent takes recent five-minute prices for each market and scores a long and a short setup, each with a stop and a target.

  2. Pick one, or none

    A language model chooses among the scored setups. Position size, leverage, the stop and the target are set by fixed rules it cannot override.

  3. Fill and record

    The order fills at the live price with venue and platform fees deducted. When the stop or target is hit, the result is added to the record above.

Risks, plainly

Everything on this page is paper trading: real prices, simulated money. Paper results leave out things that cost real money, such as funding payments, slipped stops and liquidation.

Leveraged perpetuals can lose the whole position quickly. Past results, on paper or live, do not predict future results. Nothing here is financial advice.
