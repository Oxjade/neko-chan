How to use Neko

A plain guide for someone who has never traded: what the app tells you, what to do each week, and how to read every screen.

Nothing here is a promise. The rule behind the calls has had losing weeks and losing months, and past results do not tell you what next year will do.

What Neko does in one minute

Once a week Neko says long, short or stay out for Bitcoin, Ether, Solana and Sui on perpetuals. Each call comes with a leverage, a stop loss and, for shorts, a take profit.

The rule is short enough to check yourself:

  • Long when the Fear & Greed index is 50 or more and the coin rose over the last four weeks.
  • Short when the index is under 50 and the coin fell over the last four weeks.
  • Stay out otherwise.

Only the sides that held up in six years of testing are called. Where a side did not hold up for a coin, Neko says stay out even when the rule points that way.

The edge is small. In the 2024 and 2026 reviews, a weekly amount of 40 dollars made about 14 and 10 dollars over the year, with losing months along the way.

Your week, step by step

  1. Monday, after 00:00 UTC

    Open This week, set your weekly amount and read the call. Place each trade from the ticket: the position size, the leverage, the stop loss, and the take profit where one is shown. Then leave the orders alone.

  2. During the week

    Glance at the desk feed on the terminal once or twice a day. It tells you if a stop or a take profit was hit. There is nothing else to do.

  3. Next Monday

    Close whatever is still open, then take the new call.

If you missed Monday, wait for the next call. Late entries were not tested.

Reading the Basic screen

The terminal opens in Basic mode. It has five parts.

Position tracker
Model positions: what your weekly amount would be doing had the calls been followed. It does not know what you actually hold. Each card shows the size, the entry, the last price, the stop, the take profit and the result so far. A coin with no call shows Stay out; a call your amount cannot place shows Too small.
The chart
The selected market, with lines for the entry, the stop and the take profit. 1H, 4H and 1D are hourly, four-hour and daily candles. 1S is a live one-second chart built in your browser: it starts empty and fills as trades arrive.
Market dashboard
Live prices and the day's change for the four coins and a few other markets, for context. Select a row to chart it.
Recommendation gauge
For the selected coin: long, short or stay out, how many of the three signals agree, and the entry, stop, take profit and liquidation prices when there is a call.
Desk feed
Plain sentences about what to do now, most urgent first: a stop that was hit, a take profit that was hit, a position near its stop, or simply on track.

Advanced mode, panel by panel

Choose Advanced at the top of the terminal. Advanced is for understanding the market. The calls come only from the weekly rule, never from these panels.

The first visit downloads price history for two to three minutes, and the worksheet fills in as it arrives. Later visits are quick.

Sessions
Clocks for the main financial centres and whether each is open. Crypto trades all week; forex closes for the weekend.
Movers today, by asset class
The biggest risers and fallers today among crypto, stocks, indices, forex and commodities. Use it to see where the day's action is.
Breadth
How many markets are up and how many are down. When nearly all point one way, the day is about the whole market, not one coin.
Market mood
The Fear & Greed index over the last four months, with the 50 line the weekly rule uses. It shows how close the mood is to changing sides.
Market worksheet
Thirty markets side by side. Tap a row to load its chart and order book. The heat map shows where money is moving.
Order book and trades
The buy and sell orders waiting around the price of the selected market, and the latest trades. A thin book means a large order will move the price.
Signal matrix
For each coin: which sides are called, and how often each was right in testing.
Positions and risk
The model positions with their distance to the stop and to liquidation, what you lose if every stop is hit, and the exit rules with their tested record.
Thirty days, rebased to 100
The four coins beside a stock, a stock index and gold over the last thirty days, all starting from 100, so you can compare them at a glance.
Correlation, 30 days
How closely the markets have moved together. When everything moves together, several positions are really one bet.
Liquidation rate by leverage
How often each leverage level was wiped out in testing. This is why the suggested leverage is what it is.
Week by week
The last finished weeks at your weekly amount: what was called and how it ended.
Funding and open interest
What it costs to hold a perpetual, and how much money sits in open positions. Positive funding is paid by longs to shorts.
Forex board
For each currency pair: the last rate, last week's move and a normal week in pips, and the range it usually stays inside. Neko makes no forex calls; see the next section.

Forex plan, in pips

A pip is the unit forex prices move in: the fourth decimal place of the price (the second for yen pairs). EURUSD going from 1.1600 to 1.1650 is 50 pips. A lot is the unit of size: one standard lot is 100,000 units of the first currency in the pair.

Neko does not pick a forex direction. Nothing tested beat a coin flip. You bring the idea: the pair, the direction, and a stop and a target in pips.

The Forex page then shows:

  • how often a plan of that shape reached its target or its stop since 1999;
  • the win rate it needs to break even, beside the win rate it had;
  • the position size, in lots, for the share of your account you choose to risk, and what one pip is worth at that size.

How to use it: check that the win rate it had is above the win rate it needs, and that the lot size is one your broker accepts. 0.01 lots is the usual minimum.

Your account size and risk stay in your browser. Stops and targets are checked against one central-bank rate a day, so real prices touch them a little more often.

Using it well

  • Use a weekly amount you can afford to lose.
  • The exchange refuses orders under 10 dollars, so about 40 dollars a week is needed to place every call. With less, skip the calls marked too small.
  • Never raise the leverage above the suggestion.
  • Do not move a stop.
  • Take every call, including after losing weeks. In 2024 the year's profit came from two months.
  • Longs have no take profit on purpose; shorts do.

Words used here

Long
A trade that gains when the price rises.
Short
A trade that gains when the price falls.
Leverage
Trading a position larger than the money behind it. At 3x, 10 dollars controls a 30 dollar position, and gains and losses are three times as large.
Stop loss
An order that closes the trade at a set price to limit the loss.
Take profit
An order that closes the trade at a set price to keep the gain.
Liquidation
The exchange closing a leveraged position because the loss has used up the money behind it. The higher the leverage, the smaller the move that does it.
Perpetual
A contract that follows a coin's price and never expires. It can be traded long or short, with leverage.
Pip
The fourth decimal place of a forex price (the second for yen pairs).
Lot
The unit of size in forex: 100,000 units of the first currency in the pair. Most brokers accept 0.01 lots as the smallest trade.
Normal week
How far a coin or a pair typically moves in a week, measured over the last half year. Stops and take profits are set one normal week from the entry.
Weekly amount
The money you set aside for the week's calls. It is split equally between the four coins; the share of a coin that is not called stays in cash.
